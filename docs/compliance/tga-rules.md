# TGA and therapeutic goods advertising

TGA rules concern therapeutic goods and their promotion. A business may need to consider them alongside regulated health service advertising requirements. The product, claims, presentation and surrounding context matter.

Use the [prescription medicine guide](../health-and-abe/prescription-medicines.md) for direct and indirect medicine promotion. Changing a brand name to an ingredient is not a general workaround.

For products near the cosmetic boundary, a claim about appearance and a claim about changing physiology can lead to different regulatory questions. Do not classify an entire product from one isolated phrase. Check [TGA's cosmetic-or-therapeutic guidance](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/determining-if-your-product-cosmetic-or-therapeutic-good).

Testimonials for therapeutic goods have their own conditions; they are not all governed by an identical blanket ban. See [testimonials and images](../health-and-abe/testimonials-and-images.md).
