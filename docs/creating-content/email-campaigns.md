# Prepare an email campaign

Email work starts with the audience and purpose. Agree the sender, intended recipients, lawful basis for contact, subject, message and desired action. Use the client's approved list and sending system; do not treat a collection of public email addresses as an approved campaign audience.

## Review the complete message

- Confirm names, dates, offers, links and any exclusions.
- Review the sender and reply destination.
- Check the required unsubscribe and identification arrangements in the sending system.
- Send an authorised internal test where the engagement includes one.
- Approve the final recipient selection and content before sending.

NRS's report-delivery email is a specific workflow. It is not proof of a general bulk email campaign engine or a universal consent check. Campaign tooling and delivery responsibilities are agreed in the engagement.

After sending, distinguish the provider's acceptance from delivery, opens and a business result. For a report to an existing client, follow [report email delivery](../team-and-sharing/email-reports.md). Do not include patient information in a marketing brief or support screenshot.


## Prepare a neutral Google review invitation

Where this preview is available, open [Google profile](https://www.notrealsmart.com.au/console/business-profile), find the correct business and connected Google account, then choose **Prepare review invitation**. NRS reads that account's current location and review link before showing fixed invitation copy. It says **Nothing was sent**. You can read and select the copy; this does not create an email campaign or select any recipients.

Your connected AI can prepare the same preview. Name the business and Google account; the AI handles the account reference. It uses your existing business access. Preparing this preview does not choose recipients, accept custom wording, replace the review link or authorise sending.

Invite people with a genuine experience, without selecting only those who were happy. Do not offer incentives or ask for a particular rating. NRS uses the review link supplied by that owned Google profile; it does not independently open its destination or establish that the listing is verified. A missing link, changed access or unavailable reading produces no usable invitation.

For a health service, review the returned conditional guidance about clinical testimonials, advertising context and privacy. The preview does not certify a campaign's legality and asks people not to share private information. Google's [review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) and [content policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114) explain genuine invitations and manipulation restrictions. Ahpra's [advertising guidance](https://www.ahpra.gov.au/Resources/Advertising-hub/Advertising-guidelines-and-other-guidance/Advertising-guidelines.aspx), December 2020 sections 4.3.1–4.3.2, distinguishes different review contexts.

Review replies remain in Inbox. Sending a new invitation is a separate outcome: agree the exact recipients, channel, message and link, then use an authorised sending process with provider readback. This preview and its fingerprint do not approve that delivery, and NRS's report-email function must not be used as a substitute.
